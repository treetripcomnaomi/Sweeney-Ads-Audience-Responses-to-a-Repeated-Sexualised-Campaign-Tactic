## An Example of Accessing Audience Responses to a 'Repeated' Controversial Advertising tactic
### A comparative analysis of YouTube comments on Sydney Sweeney’s American Eagle and Novig campaigns.

## Overview

How do audiences respond to controversial advertising—and how well does automated sentiment analysis capture their positions?

This project examines audience responses across two advertising campaigns, distinguishing emotional tone from stance toward the advertisements. It also considers who comments target and whether they express moral criticism, recognise marketing tactics, dismiss the controversy, or defend the campaign.

## Why This Matters

A negative comment may attack an advertisement’s critics while defending the advertisement itself. Conversely, a sarcastic comment may express opposition using apparently positive language.

This distinction matters for interpreting social media backlash and evaluating what sentiment scores can reveal about audience responses.

## Analytical Approach

The research design combines:

Comparison of comments within matched 45-day campaign windows.

Sentiment analysis using VADER, with TextBlob as a comparison.

Annotation of stance, comment target, and response mode.

Keyword analysis and exploratory topic modelling.

An exploratory examination of later comments connecting the campaigns.

## Key takeaways:

Why Go Beyond Sentiment?
Negative language does not necessarily indicate opposition to an advertisement. A commenter may use hostile language to attack the advertisement’s critics while defending the campaign. Conversely, sarcasm may express criticism through apparently positive wording.

This project therefore distinguishes sentiment (emotional tone), stance (support or opposition toward the advertising portrayal), and comment target (who or what is being discussed). This approach helps assess whether negative sentiment actually reflects advertising backlash.
